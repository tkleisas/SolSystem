## What the numbers say

- **The baseline is viable but thin.** The minimum food reserve is 30.0 days (the trade holds every site at its 30-day target), but the minimum water is only 5.1 days and the minimum parts stock reaches 0 t: Meridian's water and parts are the real weak points, not its food.
- **The flare is a power problem, not a food problem.** It produces 5808 MWh of unserved load and 7 blackout site-days; the battery carries the first days and the farm is curtailed, which is exactly the resilience case for the farm and the storage.
- **The blockade is the food problem.** It produces 51 hunger site-days: Meridian's 18 t/day farm cannot feed 60 t/day of demand, so the station depends on the Earth route. The farm is resilience, not self-sufficiency, until it is much larger.
- **The crop failure** costs 0 hunger site-days: the 30-day reserve and the Earth route absorb a 50 % farm loss, which is the model saying the imported food is the buffer.
- **The recycler failure** shows a small water effect: the station holds two days of water and the closure loss is only 20 t/day at 99.5 %.
- **The AI outage is a process-control failure, not a comfort problem.** It cuts the recycler's closure and the farm's output: the minimum water reserve falls to 1.0 days, the thinnest single-disruption result in the set. The AI is metered energy because it is the process control.

## The viability verdict

The model is **viable as a game**: the constraints bind in the right order — energy first under a flare,
food first under a blockade — and the trade routes are the difference between a thin station and a
hungry one. It is **not viable as a self-sufficient station**: Meridian's farm covers 18 of its 60 t/day,
and the 99.5 % recycler needs the Earth make-up for water and nutrients even when the power is fine.

The next iteration should either enlarge the farm (0.84 km², 361 MW, ~72× the import cost) or accept
that the station is a port, not a closed loop, and price the Earth route into the contract board.
